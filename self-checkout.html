WALMART CANADA · 2026

Make the final touchpoint seamless.

Self-checkout is Walmart’s final opportunity to create a seamless, positive touchpoint with the customer before they leave the store.

the more clarity,

  • When time matters, every word must earn its place.

  • Keep the experience simple and seamless for every shopper.
Scope
Account connection, produce lookup & payment
Methods
Ethnographic observation, qualitative interviews, usability & A/B testing

Opportunity

From a business perspective, self-checkout presents two key opportunities:

  • Leveraging customer purchase and shopping behavior to connect to a broader Walmart profile, services, and omnichannel experiences.
  • Enabling a fast, intuitive experience that reduces lines and post-shop friction.

01 — Account connection

Why aren’t customers connecting at the moment of checkout?

Connecting at checkout is an opportunity to build a holistic customer profile and engage customers in an omni-channel system.

Research showed that the current experience did not make account connection clear or worthwhile enough for customers.

What we learned

  1. Customers were not aware of what Walmart ID is.
  2. Customers were not aware of the benefits—or the benefits were not worth it to them—to connect.
  3. Connecting accounts at the kiosk was too complex—there were too many steps involved.

The current coupon journey

Redeeming manufacturer coupons with Walmart ID asked customers to:

  1. Download the app.
  2. Create an account.
  3. Clip coupons in the app.
  4. Find the relevant items in the store.
  5. Open the app at checkout.
  6. Scan the in-app QR code.

What we explored

Account connection

  • Auto-application of available coupons post-identification to drive greater adoption.
  • Additional identification methods—phone number and QR code on screen.
  • Scan & Go as an opportunity for increased identification.

Account creation

  • Quicker account creation and immediate benefit redemption through temporary account creation.
  • Touchpoint-relevant offerings, such as cost savings from app connection for the current transaction.

Tradeoffs & limitations

  • Manufacturer agreements
  • Benefit offerings, constrained by razor-thin margins
  • Timelines that did not align with the Scan & Go rollout

Where we landed

  • Temporary account creation and connection via phone number and QR code
  • Updated visual branding and benefit education
  • Automatic card identification

02 — Produce identification

What is preventing self-checkout from being seamless, fast and intuitive for customers?

Through ethnographic observations and qualitative interviews, we identified produce identification and payment as key moments where customers struggled.

The biggest challenges

  • Knowing or guessing the exact item name, particularly for bilingual customers
  • Understanding how the item is categorized when using category-based search
  • Alternative identification methods, like code lookup, were not widely known or discoverable

Solves we tested

  • Keyboard-first versus category-first search
  • Within category-based search, isolated ranges versus name-based ranges as anchor points
  • Barcode scanning
  • Computer vision

What we found

The results showed that customers were often not confident typing a produce name, even when more name options were supported. The results were mixed between isolated ranges and anchor points.

Where we landed

Category-first search, a popular-items page, and search optimization through fuzzy search, popular terms, and popular multi-lingual naming.

03 — Payment

The team had removed the need to select a payment option before moving forward in the resolution path. However, we still needed to display payment options for edge cases like multi-card payment.

Customers’ inherent mental model was to review action components before proceeding. They spent time reading the available actions, only to find none applied to their use case and that they simply needed to proceed to the pin pad with their payment type.

What we tested

We tested simplifying the experience further to make optional actions more clear, and the opposite approach: making all payment methods tappable options to proceed to the next step while allowing customers who knew they could skip the step to go straight to the pin pad.

What we found

The results showed that clear, direct payment options made the next step easier to understand.

Recommendation

  • Show users the available payment options right away, as directly as possible.
  • Rethink labeling of “gift card” purchase and reload.
  • More clearly communicate and integrate “multi-card payment” as a payment option.

DESIGN PRINCIPLES

  • Intuitive and predictable
